How One Complete Triangular Wave Cycle Works

For each channel, one complete triangular cycle involves:

1. Rising edge: CCR goes from 0 → 4999
2. Falling edge: CCR goes from 4999 → 0

With step = 1:

- Rising: 0, 1, 2, 3, ..., 4999 = 4999 interrupts
- Falling: 4999, 4998, 4997, ..., 0 = 4999 interrupts
- Total interrupts per cycle: 4999 + 4999 = 9998 interrupts

Time per cycle: 9998 ÷ 10,000 = 0.9998 seconds ≈ 1 second
Frequency: 1 ÷ 1 = 1 Hz

With step = 15:

- Rising: 0, 15, 30, 45, ..., 4995 = 333 interrupts(4995÷15 = 333)
- Note: Actually reaches 4995, not exactly 4999, but close enough
- Falling: 4995, 4980, 4965, ..., 0 = 333 interrupts
- Total interrupts per cycle: 333 + 333 = 666 interrupts

Time per cycle: 666 ÷ 10,000 = 0.0666 seconds
Frequency: 1 ÷ 0.0666 = 15 Hz per channel

Why You Get 30Hz Total:

Your code alternates between two channels:
- Channel 1: 15 Hz triangular wave
- Channel 2: 15 Hz triangular wave
- Combined output: You see a triangular wave every 1/(15×2) = 30 Hz

General Formula:

Frequency = Timer_Frequency × Step ÷ (2 × Period)

Where:
- Timer_Frequency = 10,000 Hz
- Step = your step size
- Period = 4999 (approximately 5000)
- Factor of 2 accounts for up AND down ramp

Examples:
- step = 1: 10,000 × 1 ÷ (2 × 5000) = 1 Hz per channel → 2 Hz total
- step = 15: 10,000 × 15 ÷ (2 × 5000) = 15 Hz per channel → 30 Hz total
- step = 30: 10,000 × 30 ÷ (2 × 5000) = 30 Hz per channel → 60 Hz total

For Exact 30Hz Total (15Hz per channel):

Required step = (Desired_Freq_per_channel × 2 × Period) ÷ Timer_Frequency
Required step = (15 × 2 × 5000) ÷ 10,000 = 15

That's why step = 15 gives you exactly 30Hz!
